1. Overview & Structural Breakdown

The Constitution of India is the longest written constitution of any sovereign nation in the world. At its inception in 1949, it contained 395 Articles organized into 22 Parts and 8 Schedules. Through various constitutional amendments over the decades, it now consists of over 470+ Articles in 25 Parts and 12 Schedules.

It imparts constitutional supremacy (not parliamentary supremacy) since it was created by a constituent assembly and adopted by its people with a solemn declaration.

2. The Preamble of India

Text of the Preamble

"WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:

JUSTICE, social, economic and political;
LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;
EQUALITY of status and of opportunity; and to promote among them all;
FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation;

IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION."

Note: The words "SOCIALIST", "SECULAR", and "INTEGRITY" were added to the Preamble through the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act of 1976.

3. Prominent Parts of the Constitution

The Constitution is divided into various sections covering executive, judicial, and legislative authorities:

  • Part I: The Union and its Territory (Articles 1 – 4)
  • Part II: Citizenship (Articles 5 – 11)
  • Part III: Fundamental Rights (Articles 12 – 35)
  • Part IV: Directive Principles of State Policy (Articles 36 – 51)
  • Part IV-A: Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)
  • Part V: The Union Government (Articles 52 – 151)
    • Chapter I: The Executive
    • Chapter II: Parliament
    • Chapter IV: The Union Judiciary (Supreme Court)
  • Part XVIII: Emergency Provisions (Articles 352 – 360)
  • Part XX: Amendment of the Constitution (Article 368)

4. Fundamental Rights (Part III) vs. Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A)

Six Fundamental Rights Guarantees:

  1. Right to Equality (Articles 14–18): Includes equality before law, prohibition of discrimination, and abolition of untouchability (Article 17).
  2. Right to Freedom (Articles 19–22): Guarantees freedom of speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, and profession.
  3. Right against Exploitation (Articles 23–24): Prohibits human trafficking, forced labor, and child labor in hazardous places.
  4. Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25–28): Provides freedom of conscience and free profession, practice, and propagation of religion.
  5. Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29–30): Protection of language, script, and culture of minorities.
  6. Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32): Dr. Ambedkar called Article 32 the "Heart and Soul of the Constitution" as it empowers citizens to approach the Supreme Court directly for enforcement of rights via Writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Certiorari, Prohibition, Quo-Warranto).

Key Fundamental Duties (Article 51A):

Originally 10 duties were incorporated; the 11th duty was added by the 86th Amendment in 2002. Every citizen is expected to:

  • Abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals, National Flag, and National Anthem.
  • Cherish and follow the noble ideals that inspired the national struggle for freedom.
  • Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India.
  • Promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood among all people.
  • Safeguard public property and abjure violence.

5. Drafting Committee & Historical Timeline

The Constituent Assembly took almost three years (2 years, 11 months, and 18 days) to complete the historic task of drafting the Constitution.

The Seven Members of the Drafting Committee:

  1. Dr. B. R. Ambedkar — Chairman (Widely recognized as the "Father of the Indian Constitution")
  2. N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar
  3. Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar
  4. Dr. K. M. Munshi
  5. Syed Mohammad Saadulla
  6. N. Madhava Rau (replaced B. L. Mitter)
  7. D. P. Khaitan (replaced by T. T. Krishnamachari in 1948)

Chronological Drafting Timeline

9th December 1946
First meeting of the Constituent Assembly. Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha was appointed temporary President.
11th December 1946
Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected as the permanent President of the Assembly.
13th December 1946
Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru moved the historic 'Objectives Resolution'.
29th August 1947
Drafting Committee appointed under the chairmanship of Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.
26th November 1949
The Constitution was formally passed and adopted by the Constituent Assembly. Celebrated as Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas).
26th January 1950
The Constitution came into force across the country, turning India into a Republic (celebrated as Republic Day).

6. Major Constitutional Amendments

Article 368 details the procedure to amend the Constitution, maintaining a balance between rigidity and flexibility.

Amendment Act Year Key Subject / Change Introduced
1st Amendment 1951 Added the Ninth Schedule to protect land reform laws from judicial review.
42nd Amendment 1976 Known as the "Mini-Constitution". Added 'Socialist, Secular, Integrity' to Preamble and introduced Fundamental Duties.
44th Amendment 1978 Removed Right to Property from Fundamental Rights and made it a constitutional right under Article 300A.
73rd & 74th Amendment 1992 Granted constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions (Rural) and Municipalities (Urban).
86th Amendment 2002 Made free and compulsory education a Fundamental Right for children aged 6 to 14 under Article 21A.
101st Amendment 2016 Introduced the Goods and Services Tax (GST) across India.

7. Frequently Asked Questions (Interactive HTML5 Details)

Click on any question below to reveal the answer:

Who was the calligraphy artist of the original Constitution?

The original Constitution of India was handwritten by Prem Behari Narain Raizada in a flowing italic style with beautiful calligraphy, without taking any remuneration.

Where are the original handwritten copies kept today?

The original handwritten Hindi and English copies are preserved in special helium-filled glass cases inside the Library of the Parliament of India in New Delhi.

Why was 26th January chosen as Republic Day?

26th January was chosen because it was on this day in 1930 that the Indian National Congress proclaimed the Declaration of Indian Independence (Poorna Swaraj).

How many constitutional amendments have taken place so far?

As of recent legislative records, there have been 106 amendments made to the Constitution of India since 1950.
